## 1. Investment Recommendation

I recommend that the investment committee place Netflix (NFLX)
on its watchlist rather than initiate a position at its
January 23, 2026 closing price of $86.12.

My five-year discounted cash flow (DCF) analysis estimates
Netflix's standalone intrinsic value between $42.13 and
$60.33 per share, with a base-case valuation of $51.20.

The historical market price was approximately 68% above
my base-case estimate, suggesting that investors were
pricing in stronger future performance than my
operating assumptions support.

Even under my high operating scenario, Netflix's estimated
intrinsic value remains below its market price.

This recommendation reflects a historical investment
decision, not an October 2026 trading recommendation.

The pending Warner Bros. Discovery (WBD) acquisition is
excluded from the standalone valuation. Its financing,
dilution, integration costs, and potential synergies require
separate evaluation before an investment decision.

## 2. Historical Performance and Financial Forecast

My analysis uses a five-year financial model integrating
Netflix's income statement, balance sheet, and cash flow
statement.

I normalized FY2025 operating income to account for the
$619 million cumulative charge related to Netflix's
Brazilian tax dispute.

This adjustment increased reported EBIT from approximately
$13.33 billion to $13.95 billion.

I removed the identified cumulative charge from recurring
forecast costs because I do not assume an expense of this
magnitude will recur annually.

However, the model retains approximately $700 million in
expected FY2026 deposits associated with the dispute.

This distinction allows the model to normalize operating
profitability without disregarding expected cash outflows.

The adjustment remains a judgment because some Brazil-related
tax obligations may continue.

My base case assumes annual revenue growth moderates from
12% in FY2026 to 6% in FY2030, reflecting the challenge
of sustaining high percentage growth as Netflix expands.

Content additions are projected to grow between 8% and
4% annually over the same period.

The model also assumes operating expenses decline from
37.5% to 34.5% of gross profit as Netflix benefits
from operating efficiencies.

By FY2030, the model projects:

| Financial Metric | FY2030 Projection |
|---|---:|
| Revenue | $68.19 billion |
| Operating Income | $24.11 billion |
| Operating Margin | 35.4% |
| Free Cash Flow to the Firm | $18.00 billion |

The projected operating margin of 35.4% is an important
assumption to challenge.

Achieving this level of profitability depends on Netflix
continuing to grow revenue while controlling content and
other operating expenses.

If content spending remains elevated or revenue growth
slows more quickly than expected, future cash flows
could fall below the base-case forecast.

## 3. Discounted Cash Flow Valuation

I valued Netflix using projected free cash flow to the
firm (FCFF), discounted at a 9% weighted average cost
of capital (WACC).

The base case assumes 3% terminal growth and 15%
terminal return on invested capital (ROIC).

The 15% terminal ROIC implies reinvestment equal
to 20% of terminal net operating profit after tax
to support long-term growth.

### Base-Case Valuation

| Component | Value |
|---|---:|
| Present Value of Forecast FCFF | $56.40 billion |
| Present Value of Terminal Value | $170.02 billion |
| Enterprise Value | $226.42 billion |
| Eligible Cash and Investments | +$9.06 billion |
| Debt | -$14.46 billion |
| Equity Value | $221.02 billion |
| **Estimated Value per Share** | **$51.20** |

The per-share valuation uses approximately 4.32 billion
shares, including an estimated dilution allowance.

An important limitation is that terminal value accounts
for approximately 75.1% of enterprise value.

This makes Netflix's valuation particularly sensitive
to long-term growth, reinvestment, profitability,
and discount-rate assumptions.

The 9% WACC incorporates a 4.24% Treasury yield
and assumed values for beta, the equity risk premium,
and borrowing costs.

These assumptions require financial judgment and
should not be interpreted as independently verified
Netflix-specific market estimates.

## 4. Scenario and Sensitivity Analysis

I evaluated alternative operating conditions to
determine how changes in Netflix's performance
could affect its intrinsic value.

| Scenario | Estimated Value per Share |
|---|---:|
| Low Operating Case | $42.13 |
| Base Operating Case | $51.20 |
| High Operating Case | $60.33 |
| Historical Market Price | $86.12 |

The low case incorporates slower revenue growth,
higher content spending, and weaker operating leverage.

The high case assumes stronger growth and greater
operating efficiency.

Even under the high operating scenario, Netflix's
estimated value remains below the historical
market price, supporting WATCH–DEFER.

A separate WACC and terminal-growth sensitivity grid
produces estimated values ranging from $41.73 to $69.45.

I also evaluated a structural impairment scenario,
which produces a value of approximately $15.57 per share.

This scenario illustrates the potential downside
if Netflix experiences sustained weakness in
growth and profitability.

### Additional WACC Validation Test

To evaluate the sensitivity of the DCF to the
required rate of return, I increased WACC
from 9% to 12% while holding other independent
assumptions constant.

| Metric | 9% WACC | 12% WACC |
|---|---:|---:|
| Intrinsic Value per Share | $51.20 | $33.71 |
| Enterprise Value | $226.42B | $150.89B |
| PV of Terminal Value | $170.02B | $98.96B |

Increasing WACC reduced estimated intrinsic
value by approximately 34.2%.

This demonstrates that Netflix's valuation is
highly sensitive to cost-of-capital assumptions.

The test also reinforces the importance of
independently evaluating beta, the equity
risk premium, and borrowing costs.

Although the valuation decreased substantially,
the investment recommendation remained
WATCH–DEFER because both estimates are below
the historical market price.

The 12% WACC is a sensitivity scenario rather
than a replacement for the 9% base assumption.

## 5. Reverse DCF and Comparable Valuation

I conducted a reverse DCF to estimate the
revenue growth required to justify Netflix's
historical market price of $86.12.

Holding other independent assumptions constant,
the model requires approximately 17.16%
annualized revenue growth through FY2030.

This would result in approximately $99.76
billion in FY2030 revenue, substantially
above my base-case projection.

The reverse DCF illustrates the stronger
operating performance necessary to support
the market price under the tested assumptions.

I also used Disney as a qualified comparable
company to evaluate Netflix using P/E
and EV/Revenue multiples.

| Method | Implied NFLX Value |
|---|---:|
| Disney P/E | $40.99/share |
| Disney EV/Revenue | $25.27/share |
| Base-Case DCF | $51.20/share |

Disney is an imperfect comparable because
its diversified operations include theme parks,
streaming, film, and other entertainment businesses.

Differences in profitability, growth expectations,
and capital requirements limit the usefulness
of applying Disney's multiples directly to Netflix.

For this reason, I use the comparable valuations
as secondary checks rather than mechanically
averaging them with the DCF.

## 6. Investment Risks and Monitoring

The most important risks to my valuation include:

- Content expenditures growing faster than revenue.
- Operating margins failing to reach forecast levels.
- Higher WACC or lower long-term returns on capital.
- Additional Brazil-related tax obligations.
- WBD acquisition financing, dilution, and integration costs.
- Forecast uncertainty and reliance on terminal value.

I would reconsider initiating a position at approximately
$33.70 per share or lower, representing a proposed
20% margin of safety relative to the low operating-case
valuation of $42.13.

However, a lower stock price alone would not
justify an investment.

Before initiating a position, I would require
evidence that Netflix's operating performance
remains consistent with the underlying valuation
assumptions and that the WBD transaction has been
evaluated separately.

I would monitor quarterly revenue growth,
content investment, operating margins,
free cash flow, Brazil-related obligations,
and transaction developments.

Revenue growth below 10% in the first forecast
year would trigger a reassessment of the base case.

A persistent period of minimal growth, elevated
content spending, and returns below the cost
of capital could support a DO-NOT-INITIATE decision.

## 8. Conclusion

My standalone valuation suggests that Netflix's
intrinsic value as of January 23, 2026 was below
its historical market price.

The base-case DCF estimates $51.20 per share,
with an operating-case range of $42.13–$60.33.

Scenario analysis, comparable-company valuation,
and reverse DCF indicate that the $86.12 market
price requires stronger future performance
than my base-case assumptions support.

Although Netflix has the potential to generate
significant future cash flows, I do not believe
the standalone valuation justifies initiating
a position at the historical market price.

**Final Recommendation: WATCH–DEFER.**

I would reconsider the investment if Netflix's
share price became more attractive relative
to supported intrinsic value or new financial
evidence justified stronger operating assumptions.

A final initiation decision would also require
separate analysis of the pending WBD acquisition.
