# Project 1 — Edition A: Netflix, Inc. (NFLX)

## Decision and user

**Decision:** **Watch–defer. Do not initiate a position yet.**

**User:** The fund's investment committee. This memo supports an initial screening decision: whether Netflix merits full underwriting for a potential investment. It is not yet a buy recommendation or a completed valuation.

## Company and valuation date

- **Company:** Netflix, Inc. (NASDAQ: NFLX; CIK 0001065280)
- **Valuation date:** January 23, 2026, the filing date of the FY2025 Form 10-K
- **Information boundary:** This Edition A uses information available in the FY2025 Form 10-K. A valuation-date share price and market-implied expectations have not yet been verified, so no price target or expected return is claimed.

## Initial thesis

Netflix appears to be a high-quality, scaled entertainment platform whose 2025 revenue growth converted into faster operating-income and operating-cash-flow growth. The preliminary business evidence supports further research. However, a good business is not automatically a good investment: without a verified market price, normalized forward earnings, and a valuation range, the committee cannot determine whether the prospective return compensates for content, competition, foreign-exchange, execution, and transaction risks. The appropriate initial action is therefore **watch–defer while completing valuation and risk underwriting**.

## Known evidence

All dollar figures below are U.S. dollars. Financial-statement amounts are converted from the filing's reported units of thousands.

1. **Revenue grew 16% to $45.183 billion in 2025 from $39.001 billion in 2024.** Management attributes the increase primarily to membership growth, price increases, and increased advertising revenue, partly offset by unfavorable foreign exchange. Monthly membership fees remained the primary revenue source. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, Revenues](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1065280/000106528026000034/nflx-20251231.htm)

2. **Operating income increased 28% to $13.327 billion, and operating margin expanded to 29.5% from 26.7%.** Revenue therefore grew faster than relevant operating costs in 2025. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 7 and Note 13](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1065280/000106528026000034/nflx-20251231.htm)

3. **Operating cash flow increased 38% to $10.149 billion.** The increase was helped by higher net income and noncash adjustments, partly offset by higher content payments and unfavorable working-capital changes. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Liquidity and Capital Resources](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1065280/000106528026000034/nflx-20251231.htm)

4. **Liquidity and leverage remain material to valuation.** At year-end, Netflix reported $9.034 billion of cash and cash equivalents and $14.463 billion of short- and long-term debt on a carrying-value basis, implying $5.429 billion of simple net debt before other adjustments. Note 7 separately reports $14.519 billion of debt principal. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Consolidated Balance Sheets and Note 7](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1065280/000106528026000034/nflx-20251231.htm)

5. **Content commitments are economically consequential.** Total content obligations were $24.039 billion, including $18.4 billion not yet recognized on the balance sheet; $11.528 billion was due within the next 12 months. These commitments reduce near-term flexibility if operating performance weakens. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Contractual Obligations and Note 9](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1065280/000106528026000034/nflx-20251231.htm)

6. **The diluted-share base is split-adjusted.** The 2025 diluted weighted-average share count was 4.344 billion and diluted EPS was $2.53 after retroactive adjustment for the November 2025 ten-for-one stock split. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Note 3, Earnings per Share](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1065280/000106528026000034/nflx-20251231.htm)

7. **Currency exposure is meaningful.** Currencies other than the U.S. dollar accounted for 56% of 2025 revenue and 31% of operating expenses. Reported 2025 revenue was approximately $271 million below management's constant-currency measure. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 7A, Foreign Currency Risk](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1065280/000106528026000034/nflx-20251231.htm)

**Primary filing record:** Form 10-K for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2025; filed January 23, 2026; accession number **0001065280-26-000034**. [SEC EDGAR filing detail](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1065280/000106528026000034/0001065280-26-000034-index.htm)

## Consequential assumptions

- Membership growth, pricing, and advertising can sustain positive revenue growth without materially damaging engagement or retention.
- The 2025 operating-margin improvement contains a durable efficiency component rather than reflecting only content timing, release cadence, or temporary cost restraint.
- Netflix can maintain content quality while keeping content cash spending and commitments proportionate to revenue and cash generation.
- Reported operating cash flow is a useful starting point for owner cash generation, but normalization must account for content-payment timing and capital expenditures.
- Foreign-exchange headwinds do not overwhelm underlying international growth over the underwriting horizon.
- Management's capital allocation—including repurchases, debt decisions, and any major transaction—creates value per diluted share.
- The eventual entry valuation must offer an adequate margin of safety under conservative growth and margin assumptions.

## Unknowns

- The verified NFLX share price, equity value, and enterprise value on the valuation date.
- The growth, margin, and cash-flow expectations already embedded in that price.
- Forward membership, pricing, advertising, engagement, churn, and regional revenue economics at a decision-useful level.
- A normalized content cash-spending requirement and the sustainable conversion of operating income into free cash flow.
- The portion of 2025 margin expansion that is structural versus timing-related.
- The status, financing, dilution, integration costs, and value impact of any material announced transaction referenced in the filing.
- A defensible downside value under slower growth, weaker pricing power, higher content spending, or adverse currency movements.
- Relevant peer and historical valuation ranges using consistently defined metrics.

## Research plan

1. Verify the closing share price and diluted share count for a clearly specified valuation date; reconcile basic, diluted weighted-average, and period-end shares.
2. Build equity value and enterprise value, explicitly reconciling cash, investments, debt carrying value versus principal, and any other required claims or non-operating assets.
3. Reconstruct at least five years of revenue, operating margin, content additions/payments, operating cash flow, capital expenditures, and free cash flow from SEC filings.
4. Separate growth into membership, pricing, advertising, foreign exchange, and other drivers where disclosure permits; identify disclosure gaps rather than inventing estimates.
5. Normalize content economics and test cash conversion under different content-spending and working-capital assumptions.
6. Model bear, base, and bull cases with explicit revenue growth, operating margin, tax, reinvestment, and dilution assumptions.
7. Value Netflix using a DCF and cross-check it with relevant EV/revenue, EV/EBIT, P/E, and free-cash-flow-yield comparisons.
8. Investigate material post-filing developments and transaction terms using primary sources, then quantify their impact separately from the standalone case.
9. Define thesis-monitoring indicators and decision thresholds for initiating, continuing to watch, or rejecting the investment.
